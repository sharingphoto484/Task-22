COMMODITY PRICE FORECASTING ANALYSIS

PROMPT GOAL SUMMARY

The analysis objective is to perform comprehensive commodity price forecasting using three datasets containing daily weekly and monthly price observations spanning twenty nine years from 1997 through 2025. The primary goal involves implementing eight distinct statistical analyses including ARIMA time series forecasting quantile regression exponential smoothing survival analysis correlation assessment volatility comparison range ratio analysis and momentum analysis. Each analysis requires specific data preprocessing techniques model fitting parameter extraction and visualization generation to support pricing strategy optimization and risk management decisions. The analytical outputs must include precise numerical metrics rounded to specified decimal places along with corresponding production ready visualizations showing trends patterns and statistical relationships. The final deliverable should provide actionable pricing strategy recommendations based on integrated insights from ARIMA forecasting results and Holt Winters seasonal decomposition patterns.

STEP BY STEP SOLUTION

Step 1 Data Loading and Initial Exploration: The analysis begins by loading three Excel files containing daily weekly and monthly commodity price data into separate pandas dataframes for independent processing. The daily dataset contains 7253 observations with columns for Date Price Year Month and Day representing the most granular level of price information. The weekly and monthly datasets contain 1505 and 346 observations respectively providing aggregated views of price movements across different temporal resolutions.

Step 2 Daily Data Preparation for ARIMA: The daily dataframe undergoes temporal sorting by converting the Date column to datetime format and arranging all observations in ascending chronological order from earliest to most recent. This sorted dataset is then split into training and validation subsets where the first 7200 observations form the training data and the remaining 53 observations serve as validation data. The chronological ordering ensures that the model learns from historical patterns and gets tested on truly future unseen data points.

Step 3 ARIMA Model Specification and Fitting: An ARIMA model with order parameters p equals 2 d equals 1 and q equals 1 is fitted on the training price series to capture autoregressive integrated and moving average components. The parameter p equals 2 indicates two lagged price values influence current prices while d equals 1 specifies first order differencing to achieve stationarity. The parameter q equals 1 incorporates one lagged forecast error into the prediction equation creating a balanced model that captures both trend and short term fluctuations.

Step 4 Forecast Generation and Confidence Intervals: After fitting the ARIMA model on 7200 training observations the system generates forecasts for the next 30 days beyond the training period extending predictions into the validation timeframe. The forecasting process produces not only point estimates but also confidence intervals using the get forecast method which calculates upper and lower bounds based on forecast variance. These confidence intervals provide uncertainty quantification showing the range within which future prices are likely to fall with 95 percent probability.

Step 5 Model Performance Evaluation: The mean absolute error is calculated by comparing the forecasted values against actual validation prices for the overlapping 30 day period measuring average prediction deviation. The MAE of 0.27 indicates that on average the model predictions deviate from actual prices by only 27 cents demonstrating strong forecasting accuracy. This metric provides a scale dependent measure of forecast quality that analysts can directly interpret in dollar terms for business decision making.

Step 6 ARIMA Visualization Creation: A comprehensive line plot displays three distinct series including actual historical prices in blue fitted training values in green and forecasted future prices in red with time period on the x axis and price values on the y axis. The plot incorporates a shaded confidence interval region around the red forecast line showing the uncertainty band within which future prices are expected to fall. This three component visualization enables stakeholders to assess model fit quality historical pattern capture and future price trajectory simultaneously.

Step 7 Weekly Data Preparation for Quantile Regression: The weekly dataset undergoes chronological sorting by Week Start date and receives a new time index column containing sequential integers from 0 to 1504 representing observation order. This time index serves as the predictor variable in the quantile regression model transforming calendar dates into a linear numeric scale. The sequential indexing captures the underlying temporal trend while abstracting away from specific date values enabling clean slope interpretation.

Step 8 Quantile Regression Model Fitting: A quantile regression model targeting the 90th percentile is fitted with Price as the dependent variable and time index as the independent predictor to model upper price envelope evolution. Unlike ordinary least squares regression that models the conditional mean this approach models the conditional 90th percentile showing how high prices evolve over time. The model fitting uses an intercept term and the time index coefficient to create a linear equation describing the 90th percentile price trajectory.

Step 9 Time Coefficient Interpretation: The extracted time coefficient of negative 0.0024 indicates that the 90th percentile price decreases by approximately 0.0024 units per week representing a slight downward trend in the upper price boundary. This negative coefficient suggests that extreme high prices have been gradually declining over the analysis period which has important implications for pricing ceiling strategies. The four decimal precision enables detection of subtle trends that might be missed with coarser rounding.

Step 10 Quantile Regression Visualization: A scatter plot displays all weekly price observations as semi transparent points with time index on the x axis and price on the y axis overlaid with a red regression line showing the fitted 90th percentile trajectory. This visualization clearly distinguishes between the cloud of actual price points and the upper envelope modeled by the quantile regression line. The plot enables visual assessment of how well the 90th percentile line captures the upper price boundary and whether the negative trend is consistent across different time periods.

Step 11 Monthly Aggregation for Exponential Smoothing: Daily prices are aggregated to monthly frequency by grouping observations by Year and Month columns then computing the mean price within each group creating a monthly time series. This aggregation reduces the 7253 daily observations to 347 monthly observations smoothing out daily volatility while preserving medium term trends and seasonal patterns. The monthly aggregated series provides the appropriate temporal resolution for Holt Winters seasonal modeling with a 12 month seasonal period.

Step 12 Holt Winters Model Configuration: An exponential smoothing model is specified with seasonal period of 12 months to capture annual seasonality additive trend component to model linear growth or decline and additive seasonal component to represent fixed seasonal effects. The additive specification assumes that seasonal fluctuations remain constant in absolute terms rather than growing proportionally with the price level. This configuration enables simultaneous decomposition of the monthly price series into level trend seasonal and residual components.

Step 13 Alpha Parameter Extraction: The fitted model yields an alpha smoothing parameter of 1.0000 indicating maximum weight is given to the most recent observation when updating the level component. An alpha of 1.0 suggests that the model is highly responsive to new information rather than smoothing across multiple periods which occurs when historical data patterns are highly variable. This parameter value reveals that prices exhibit significant period to period changes requiring rapid adaptation rather than gradual smoothing.

Step 14 Decomposition Visualization Creation: A four panel vertical subplot arrangement displays the original observed monthly series in the top panel followed by the extracted trend component seasonal component and residual component in subsequent panels. Each subplot shares the same time axis enabling direct temporal alignment of patterns across the four components. This decomposition plot allows analysts to separately examine the underlying trend the repeating seasonal pattern and the irregular residual fluctuations that remain after accounting for trend and seasonality.

Step 15 Survival Data Construction: For survival analysis each daily price observation is evaluated against a six dollar threshold creating survival time variables that count consecutive days where price remains at or below the threshold. When price exceeds six dollars an event is recorded with the current survival time and the counter resets to zero for the next survival episode. Observations where price stays at or below six dollars are marked as censored indicating the event has not yet occurred creating a dataset of survival durations with event indicators.

Step 16 Kaplan Meier Estimation: A custom Kaplan Meier estimator calculates the survival function by computing the probability of price remaining at or below six dollars as a function of time measured in days. The estimator processes survival times in chronological order calculating the proportion of at risk observations that experience the threshold crossing event at each unique time point. The survival probability is computed as the cumulative product of one minus the hazard rate at each time yielding a step function that decreases each time an event occurs.

Step 17 Median Survival Time Calculation: The median survival time of 1 day is extracted by identifying the first time point at which the survival probability drops to or below 0.5 representing when half of survival episodes have ended. This extremely short median indicates that prices very frequently and rapidly cross the six dollar threshold suggesting high volatility around this price level. The one day median has critical implications for risk management indicating that price threshold breaches occur almost immediately rather than after extended stable periods.

Step 18 Cross Frequency Correlation Analysis: Daily prices are aggregated to monthly frequency by computing mean prices within each Year Month group creating a daily derived monthly series that is then merged with the original monthly dataset on matching Year Month keys. The Pearson correlation coefficient of 1.0000 between these two monthly series indicates perfect linear agreement confirming that the aggregation method produces consistent results. This perfect correlation validates data quality and demonstrates that daily fluctuations average out to match the independently recorded monthly price values.

Step 19 Volatility Comparison Across Frequencies: Coefficients of variation are calculated for daily weekly and monthly price series by dividing standard deviation by mean and multiplying by 100 yielding percentages of 52.73 52.48 and 51.71 respectively. The volatility ratio of 1.0198 comparing daily to monthly CV shows that daily prices are only 1.98 percent more variable than monthly prices indicating that temporal aggregation provides minimal volatility reduction. This finding suggests that price variability is driven more by persistent trends than by high frequency noise that would average out with aggregation.

Step 20 Price Range Ratio Analysis by Year: The monthly dataset is processed to extract the year component from the Month Start date column enabling grouping of price observations by calendar year for annual comparisons. For each year the minimum and maximum monthly prices are computed using aggregation functions on the grouped data capturing the price extremes within that twelve month period. The range ratio is then calculated for each year as the maximum price divided by the minimum price quantifying the proportional price swing where a value of 3.73 indicates prices fluctuated by a factor of nearly four times from trough to peak.

Step 21 Identification of Maximum Range Ratio Year: After computing range ratios for all years in the dataset the maximum value is identified along with its corresponding year to pinpoint when the commodity experienced its most extreme proportional volatility. The year 2001 emerges with the maximum range ratio of 3.73 indicating this period saw the largest relative price swings in the entire dataset spanning nearly three decades. This historical volatility marker helps analysts understand extreme market conditions and assess whether current price dynamics resemble past periods of instability.

Step 22 Price Momentum Analysis Across Time Horizons: The weekly dataset sorted chronologically is divided into two non overlapping periods representing the earliest and most recent one year windows by extracting the first 52 observations and last 52 observations respectively. The mean price is calculated separately for each 52 week period yielding a historical baseline average of 2.49 dollars from the early period and a recent average of 3.37 dollars from the latest period. The difference of 0.88 dollars obtained by subtracting the historical mean from the recent mean quantifies upward price momentum indicating sustained directional strength and long term appreciation trends.

Step 23 Pricing Strategy Recommendation Synthesis: The ARIMA forecast showing accurate short term price trajectory combined with Holt Winters decomposition revealing strong seasonal patterns and trend components provides the foundation for dynamic pricing recommendations. The analysis recommends implementing seasonal pricing adjustments that maintain price floors during identified low season troughs preventing excessive discounting while capitalizing on predictable seasonal peaks with premium pricing strategies. This data driven approach balances revenue maximization during high demand periods with market share protection during low demand periods creating an optimized pricing strategy grounded in quantitative forecasting evidence.
