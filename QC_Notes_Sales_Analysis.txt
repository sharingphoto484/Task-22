QUALITY CONTROL NOTES FOR SALES BEHAVIOR ANALYSIS

PROMPT GOAL IN 6 SENTENCES

The primary objective is to conduct a comprehensive structured evaluation of sales behavior across three temporal granularities using hourly daily and monthly datasets. The analysis requires standardization of all date formats and exclusion of any rows containing missing or nonnumeric values to ensure data integrity. When linking datasets only common dates between the files should be retained to maintain consistency in cross dataset comparisons. Eight distinct analytical stages must be executed including signal processing techniques like Hilbert transforms cubic spline fitting entropy calculations B spline smoothness indices mutual information estimation principal curve fitting and stability measurements. Each stage must produce specific numerical outputs rounded to either three or four decimal places along with corresponding visualizations showing relevant patterns. The final deliverable includes a verdict statement addressing the conditions associated with extreme analytic signal amplitudes based on the comprehensive analysis performed.

STEP BY STEP SOLUTIONS

Step 1 : Data Loading and Preparation

The first critical step involves loading all three CSV files containing sales hourly sales daily and sales monthly data into memory for processing. Each dataset contains multiple sales columns representing different product categories which must be summed to create a unified Sales metric for analysis. Date columns are standardized using consistent datetime parsing and any rows with missing or nonnumeric values are removed to ensure clean data for subsequent analytical operations.

Step 2 : Creating Hourly Sales Sequence

The hourly dataset is sorted by Date and Hour to create a continuous chronological sequence representing the complete temporal evolution of sales. This ordering is essential because signal processing techniques like the Hilbert transform require properly sequenced time series data. The sorted sequence becomes the foundation for extracting high resolution temporal dynamics and frequency domain characteristics.

Step 3 : Hilbert Transform Application

The Hilbert transform is applied to the sorted hourly sales sequence to decompose it into an analytic signal representation. This mathematical operation extracts the instantaneous amplitude envelope of the sales signal revealing underlying oscillatory patterns and intensity variations. The average of all analytic signal amplitude values is computed and rounded to four decimal places producing the first key metric of 4.3050.

Step 4 : Hourly Sales Visualization

A line chart is generated with hour index on the x axis and sales magnitude on the y axis to visualize the complete hourly sales trajectory. This visualization allows inspection of short term fluctuations peaks and troughs in the sales data. The chart serves as a high resolution view into the finest temporal granularity available in the dataset.

Step 5 : Date Alignment for Daily Hourly Comparison

Common dates between the hourly and daily datasets are identified by extracting date only components and computing their intersection. This alignment ensures that only matching temporal points are used when comparing metrics across different aggregation levels. The process prevents spurious associations that could arise from mismatched time periods.

Step 6 : Computing Maximum Hourly to Daily Ratios

For each common date the maximum hourly sales value is extracted and divided by the corresponding daily total sales. This ratio quantifies what fraction of the daily sales occurred during the peak hour revealing concentration patterns. The sequence of ratios across all dates forms a time series that captures the relationship between intraday peaks and overall daily performance.

Step 7 : Cubic Spline Fitting and Curvature Calculation

A cubic smoothing spline is fitted to the ratio sequence to model the smooth underlying trend while filtering out noise. The second derivative of this spline is computed at fine resolution points to measure how rapidly the trend is changing. The integral of squared second derivatives yields the curvature metric of 0.0000 indicating minimal nonlinear bending in the relationship.

Step 8 : Ratio Scatter Plot Generation

A scatter plot displays the ratio values across date indices with the fitted cubic spline overlaid as a smooth curve. This visualization reveals whether the max hourly to daily ratio exhibits systematic trends or remains relatively stable over time. The plot provides visual confirmation of the low curvature finding from the mathematical analysis.

Step 9 : Multiscale Block Construction

The daily sales series is divided into consecutive non overlapping blocks of exactly five days each. This partitioning creates multiple local windows for examining sales variability at a weekly scale. Only complete five day blocks are retained ensuring uniform statistical properties across all analyzed segments.

Step 10 : Entropy Calculation for Each Block

Within each five day block the sales values are normalized to sum to one creating a probability distribution. Shannon entropy is calculated for each distribution measuring the randomness or unpredictability of sales within that block. Higher entropy indicates more uniform sales distribution while lower entropy suggests concentration on specific days.

Step 11 : Mean Entropy Extraction

The entropy values from all valid five day blocks are averaged to produce a single summary statistic of 1.569 rounded to three decimals. This mean entropy represents the typical level of sales variability within weekly scale windows. The metric quantifies multiscale intensity by characterizing how dispersed or concentrated sales tend to be over short temporal horizons.

Step 12 : Entropy Boxplot Visualization

A boxplot displays the distribution of blockwise entropy values showing median quartiles and potential outliers. This visualization reveals whether entropy remains consistent across different time periods or exhibits substantial variation. The plot allows assessment of temporal stability in sales distribution patterns.

Step 13 : Monthly Sales B Spline Fitting

A B spline of degree four is fitted to the monthly sales sequence ordered chronologically by date. This high degree polynomial spline creates a smooth curve that approximates the long term trend in monthly sales. The fitted curve captures gradual seasonal patterns and trending behavior while minimizing sharp fluctuations.

Step 14 : Integrated Squared Deviation Measurement

The difference between fitted B spline values and actual observed monthly sales is squared at each time point. These squared deviations are summed across all months to produce an integrated measure of 699.989 rounded to three decimals. This metric quantifies structural smoothness by measuring how closely the smooth fitted curve approximates the observed data.

Step 15 : Monthly Sales Comparison Chart

A line chart displays both observed and B spline fitted monthly sales on the same axes with month index as x coordinate. This dual plot allows visual comparison between actual sales fluctuations and the smoothed structural trend. The visualization confirms how well the fitted curve captures the overall monthly trajectory.

Step 16 : Daily to Monthly Merger

Each daily sales record is matched with its corresponding monthly aggregate by extracting the month component from dates. This merge operation creates paired observations where each day links to the total sales of its parent month. The pairing enables cross frequency analysis between fine and coarse temporal scales.

Step 17 : Mutual Information Binning

Both daily and monthly sales values are independently binned into ten equal frequency intervals creating discrete categories. This discretization transforms continuous variables into categorical form suitable for mutual information estimation. Equal frequency binning ensures each bin contains approximately the same number of observations avoiding empty categories.

Step 18 : Cross Frequency Coupling Estimation

Mutual information is computed between the daily bins and monthly bins yielding a value of 0.1934 rounded to four decimals. This metric quantifies how much knowing the monthly sales bin reduces uncertainty about the daily sales bin. The relatively low value suggests moderate coupling between daily fluctuations and monthly aggregate patterns.

Step 19 : Hour Level Mean Calculation

Sales values are grouped by hour of day and averaged across all dates to produce mean sales for each of the 24 hours. This aggregation reveals the typical diurnal pattern showing which hours tend to have higher or lower sales. The hour level means capture systematic within day cyclical behavior.

Step 20 : Principal Curve Fitting and Distance Calculation

A polynomial approximation to a principal curve is fitted through the hour level means to model the nonlinear trajectory. The sum of squared distances between actual hour means and the fitted curve is computed yielding 17.853 rounded to three decimals. This structural divergence metric quantifies how much the hourly pattern deviates from a smooth one dimensional manifold.

Step 21 : Cross Scale Regularity via Ratio Standard Deviation

Daily sales are divided by their corresponding monthly totals for all matched date month pairs. The standard deviation of these ratios is calculated as 10.716 rounded to three decimals. This metric measures cross scale regularity by quantifying variability in how daily sales relate proportionally to their parent monthly aggregates.

Step 22 : Centered Moving Median Construction

A five day centered moving median is computed for the daily sales series where each point represents the median of a window including two days before two days after and the current day. This nonparametric smoother is robust to outliers and captures local central tendency. Only positions where full five day windows exist are retained for valid comparison.

Step 23 : Stability Index Calculation

The absolute difference between original daily sales and the centered moving median is computed at each valid position. The mean of these absolute differences is extracted as 10.362 rounded to three decimals. This stability index measures typical deviation from local median behavior quantifying day to day volatility relative to short term baseline.

Step 24 : Interpretation of Analytic Signal Amplitude Extremes

The verdict statement synthesizes findings by linking extreme analytic signal amplitudes to rapid hourly fluctuations combined with sustained high frequency variability. This interpretation recognizes that large amplitude values in the Hilbert transform occur when sales exhibit both sharp instantaneous changes and persistent oscillatory behavior. The conclusion connects the mathematical signal processing results to practical business understanding of sales volatility patterns.
