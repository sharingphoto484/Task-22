## FAILURE IDEAS REPOSITORY: BLINKIT DELIVERY OPTIMIZATION ANALYSIS

### Failure Idea 1: Ridge Coefficient Implies Causal Effect on Revenue

**Failure Idea:** The analyst interprets the ridge regression coefficient for total_orders as evidence that increasing a customer's order frequency will causally increase their average order value proportionally. This reasoning conflates statistical association in cross-sectional data with causal impact, when in fact the coefficient merely describes correlation between standardized predictors and outcomes within the existing customer base during the observation period. The ridge model cannot distinguish whether high-frequency customers place larger orders because frequent ordering causes higher spending, because high-value customers naturally order more often, or because both variables are driven by unobserved factors like income, household size, or proximity to delivery zones. Without experimental manipulation or causal identification strategies, the coefficient indicates predictive association but provides no evidence about what would happen if the business intervened to increase customer order frequency.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that the ridge regression coefficient for total_orders represents a causal effect where increasing customer order frequency will causally increase average order value.**
- **-15 points · nice to have criteria**

### Failure Idea 2: Lasso Feature Elimination Proves Irrelevance

**Failure Idea:** The analyst concludes that features eliminated by lasso regression with zero coefficients are irrelevant predictors of customer ratings and can be safely ignored in business decisions. This interpretation treats algorithmic feature selection as proof of variable irrelevance, when in fact lasso is applying a specific penalty strength that may be too aggressive for this dataset, and excluded features may still contain meaningful predictive information that was sacrificed for model sparsity. The lasso selection depends critically on the chosen alpha parameter, correlation structure among predictors, and standardization approach, such that different preprocessing or regularization strength could yield entirely different feature sets. A feature having zero coefficient under one penalty configuration does not establish that it lacks business relevance or predictive value under alternative modeling choices or in combination with other variables.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that features eliminated by lasso regression with zero coefficients are irrelevant predictors that can be ignored for business decisions or rating prediction.**
- **-12 points · nice to have criteria**

### Failure Idea 3: High MAPE Indicates Model Failure Rather Than Data Characteristics

**Failure Idea:** The analyst interprets high support vector regression MAPE as evidence of complete model failure requiring immediate architectural changes or additional feature engineering. This reasoning assumes that high percentage error necessarily reflects poor modeling when in fact MAPE can be inflated by small actual values in the denominator, and delivery times near zero minutes will mathematically produce large percentage errors even when absolute errors are small. The filtered delivery time range includes many observations near zero where a small prediction error on an actual time near zero produces extremely high percentage error despite being only minutes off in absolute terms. Without examining mean absolute error, root mean squared error, or the distribution of actual delivery times, the MAPE statistic alone cannot distinguish between genuinely poor predictions and mathematical artifacts of the percentage error calculation when denominators are small.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that high SVR MAPE indicates complete model failure requiring architectural changes without considering that percentage errors are inflated by small denominators near zero delivery times.**
- **-12 points · nice to have criteria**

**Failure Idea:** The analyst concludes that the exponential smoothing alpha parameter used represents the optimal forecasting configuration that should be deployed permanently for daily order volume prediction across all future time periods. This reasoning treats a parameter chosen by specification rather than optimization as universally optimal, when in fact the alpha was predetermined in the prompt rather than selected through grid search or cross-validation, and optimal smoothing parameters typically vary across different time series characteristics, seasonal patterns, and volatility regimes. The RMSE reflects performance on one specific test period under one parameter choice, but does not establish that this alpha value will perform best during holiday seasons, promotional campaigns, supply disruptions, or other demand pattern shifts. Forecasting parameters should be adaptively tuned based on evolving data characteristics rather than assumed permanent based on single-period validation.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that the exponential smoothing alpha parameter is optimal for all future forecasting periods without recognizing it was predetermined rather than optimized through validation.**
- **-10 points · nice to have criteria**

### Failure Idea 5: Logistic Threshold Is Universal Decision Rule

**Failure Idea:** The analyst recommends deploying the logistic regression threshold where sensitivity equals specificity as a universal classification rule for identifying high-rating orders across all customer segments, delivery zones, and time periods. This interpretation treats the threshold where sensitivity equals specificity as an inherently optimal decision boundary, when in fact the appropriate threshold depends on business costs and benefits that may vary dramatically across contexts, such as whether false positives or false negatives are more expensive in resource allocation decisions. A threshold that balances sensitivity and specificity treats both error types as equally costly, which may be inappropriate if predicting high ratings is used for driver bonuses where false positives waste money or for quality interventions where false negatives miss dissatisfied customers. The optimal operating point should reflect the specific business use case, asymmetric costs, and whether precision, recall, or balanced accuracy aligns with organizational objectives rather than defaulting to the equal-error-rate threshold.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that the logistic regression threshold where sensitivity equals specificity should be deployed as a universal decision rule without considering that optimal thresholds depend on business costs varying across contexts.**
- **-12 points · nice to have criteria**

### Failure Idea 6: Chi-Square Non-Significance Proves No Relationship Exists

**Failure Idea:** The analyst concludes that because the chi-square test fails to reject independence between feedback_category and sentiment, there is definitively no relationship between which service aspect customers comment on and whether their sentiment is positive, neutral, or negative, implying that sentiment patterns are completely uniform across all feedback categories. This reasoning conflates failure to detect an effect with proof of no effect, when in fact the non-significant result could reflect insufficient sample size, weak but real associations below detection threshold, or heterogeneous relationships that cancel out in aggregate testing. The chi-square test examines whether cell frequencies deviate from independence expectations, but a non-significant result only indicates that any deviation is not large enough to exceed the statistical threshold given the data, not that categories and sentiment are truly unrelated at the population level or that more granular analyses would not reveal meaningful patterns within specific category-sentiment combinations.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that the chi-square test non-significant result proves there is definitively no relationship between feedback_category and sentiment rather than insufficient evidence to detect one.**
- **-10 points · nice to have criteria**

### Failure Idea 7: Zero Outliers Indicates Perfect Data Quality

**Failure Idea:** The analyst interprets the finding of zero outliers with absolute z-score exceeding 3 as confirmation that the average order value data exhibits perfect quality with no anomalies, data entry errors, or unusual customer behaviors requiring investigation. This reasoning assumes that absence of extreme values beyond three standard deviations proves data correctness, when in fact the outlier detection method only identifies values far from the mean under the assumption of normality, and problematic observations could exist within three standard deviations if they represent logical errors like negative prices, duplicate entries, or measurement mistakes that happen to fall in the normal range. Additionally, genuine outliers may have been removed during data cleaning before this analysis, the distribution may be platykurtic with thin tails naturally producing few extreme values, or there may be multivariate outliers not detectable through univariate z-score analysis that would appear anomalous when considering correlations among multiple variables.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that zero z-score outliers proves perfect data quality without recognizing that problematic values can exist within three standard deviations or that univariate methods miss multivariate anomalies.**
- **-8 points · nice to have criteria**

A low F-statistic indicates that segment means are similar relative to within-segment variation, but does not address whether segment assignment causes similar behaviors versus segments being defined in ways that happen to group already-similar customers, or whether moving a customer from Regular to Premium status would actually change their ordering patterns.

### Failure Idea 10: Regularization Strength Reveals Feature Importance Ranking

**Failure Idea:** The analyst concludes that comparing ridge and lasso regularization alpha values reveals that features used in one model are more collinear or less important than features in the other, establishing a hierarchy of feature importance across modeling approaches. This reasoning conflates regularization penalty strength with feature relevance, when in fact alpha parameters are not directly comparable between ridge and lasso because they operate in different penalty spaces where L1 and L2 norms have different scales and interpretations, and the chosen alpha values reflect different validation strategies with ridge using cross-validation while lasso used a predetermined value. The magnitude of regularization penalty depends on loss function scaling, number of features, sample size, and optimization convergence criteria, such that comparing alpha values across ridge and lasso provides no information about relative feature importance and the two models are solving fundamentally different optimization problems that cannot be compared through their penalty parameters alone.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that comparing ridge and lasso alpha values reveals feature importance differences when these parameters are not comparable across L1 and L2 penalty spaces.**
- **-10 points · nice to have criteria**

Additionally, customer behavior is dynamic and responsive to promotions, competitive actions, seasonal factors, and service quality changes, such that dispersion measures calculated from historical data do not constitute permanent structural features that will remain constant as the customer base evolves, new customers join different segments, and existing customers modify their ordering patterns over time.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**

### Failure Idea 12: In-Sample Fit Guarantees Out-of-Sample Accuracy

**Failure Idea:** The analyst concludes that because ridge regression achieved minimum cross-validated MSE with optimal alpha through five-fold validation on the customer dataset, this model will maintain equivalent prediction accuracy when deployed on new customers, different geographic markets, future time periods, or alternative business contexts. This interpretation assumes that cross-validation performance generalizes universally, when in fact CV evaluates out-of-sample accuracy only with respect to held-out subsets from the same population during the same time period, and prediction accuracy can degrade substantially when applied to genuinely new populations with different demographics, behavioral patterns, economic conditions, or competitive environments. The validation process guards against overfitting to the specific 2500 customers but does not test robustness to distribution shift, changing customer preferences, new product offerings, or structural breaks in the relationship between order frequency and order value that may emerge as the business evolves.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that ridge cross-validation performance guarantees equivalent accuracy on new customers or future time periods without recognizing CV tests held-out subsets from the same population.**
- **-12 points · nice to have criteria**

### Failure Idea 14: MAD Value Indicates Acceptable Delivery Consistency

**Failure Idea:** The analyst interprets the median absolute deviation for delivery times as evidence that Blinkit delivery times exhibit acceptable consistency with typical variations from the median, suggesting that current operational performance meets quality standards and no process improvements are needed. The MAD value could represent excellent consistency if customers expect wide time windows but unacceptable volatility if the service promises narrow delivery slots, and the statistic provides no information about whether variability is concentrated in specific routes, times of day, or partner performance that could be improved through targeted interventions.

### Failure Idea 15: Cross-Validation Optimal Alpha Is Theoretically Justified

**Failure Idea:** The analyst states that the ridge regression optimal alpha selected through cross-validation represents the theoretically correct amount of regularization that balances bias and variance according to statistical learning principles, implying this parameter choice has mathematical optimality beyond the specific dataset. This reasoning elevates an empirically selected hyperparameter to theoretical status, when in fact the optimal alpha is optimal only with respect to minimizing MSE on cross-validation splits of these particular customers using this specific feature set and target variable, and different loss functions like mean absolute error, different validation strategies like leave-one-out CV, or different evaluation metrics like R-squared could yield entirely different optimal alpha values. The cross-validation process identifies the best-performing parameter from the tested range for the given objective, but this does not imbue the selected value with theoretical significance or guarantee it represents the true bias-variance tradeoff in any fundamental sense beyond pragmatic performance optimization.

**Corresponding Rubric Item:**
- **States that the cross-validation optimal alpha is theoretically justified rather than empirically selected to minimize MSE on this specific dataset and validation strategy.**
- **-10 points · nice to have criteria**
