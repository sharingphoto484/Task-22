QC NOTE FOR NBA PLAYOFF ROSTER ANALYSIS

PROMPT GOAL:

The analysis aims to model NBA playoff participation at the player team season level using regular season statistics and evaluate predictive performance through cross validation. It extracts a latent performance factor from player statistics using principal components analysis to assess how well a single composite score predicts playoff participation. The study measures roster continuity by calculating what proportion of playoff minutes are played by athletes who also participated in the regular season for the same team. It assesses the concentration of playoff playing time across team rosters using the Gini coefficient to understand inequality in minute distribution. The analysis visualizes continuity patterns across teams and years through a heatmap and tests whether recent seasons show significantly different continuity compared to earlier periods. All analyses restrict to seasons present in all three input files and handle missing data appropriately using specified weighting schemes.

STEP BY STEP SOLUTION:

Step 1: The analysis begins by loading three separate CSV files containing NBA player statistics for regular season and playoff games. The Season type field is normalized so that encoded values like Regular percent twenty Season are converted to a standard Regular Season format. This ensures consistent filtering and grouping operations across all three data sources.

Step 2: The common seasons present in all three files are identified through set intersection operations. Only these intersected years are retained for all subsequent analyses to ensure fair comparison. This restriction guarantees that every season included has complete regular season and playoff data available.

Step 3: Regular season rows are prepared for modeling by filtering to non missing values in required fields including year, player identifier, team, games played, minutes, and points. A binary label is created for each regular season player team season combination indicating whether that exact combination also appears in the playoff data. This label serves as the outcome variable for predicting playoff participation.

Step 4: Three predictors are standardized within each year by subtracting the year specific mean and dividing by the year specific standard deviation. Games played, minutes, and points are all transformed this way to remove year to year scale differences. Standardization ensures that coefficients reflect relative importance rather than being influenced by measurement units or seasonal variation.

Step 5: Cross validation folds are assigned deterministically by hashing each unique player identifier and taking modulo five. This creates five roughly equal sized folds that remain consistent across runs. Each player's records always fall in the same fold ensuring proper independence between training and validation sets.

Step 6: A logistic regression model with intercept is fit on each training fold combination using the three standardized predictors. Out of fold predictions are generated for each validation fold so every observation receives a held out probability. The cross validated area under the ROC curve is computed using these out of fold predictions to assess discrimination ability without optimistic bias.

Step 7: Principal components analysis is performed on the three standardized predictors pooled across all seasons and players. The first principal component is extracted and its loading on minutes is constrained to be positive through sign flipping if necessary. This first component represents a latent performance factor that combines games played, minutes, and points into a single composite score.

Step 8: A logistic regression is fit using only the first principal component score as the predictor of playoff participation. The variance explained by this first component quantifies how much of the total variation in the three statistics is captured by the single factor. The area under the ROC curve for this single score model measures how well the composite performance metric predicts playoff participation.

Step 9: Roster continuity is calculated for each playoff team season by identifying which playoff players also appeared in regular season for the same team and year. The sum of playoff minutes for continued players is divided by total playoff minutes for that team season. This ratio quantifies what fraction of playoff playing time goes to athletes who were with the team during regular season.

Step 10: Concentration of playoff minutes within each team season is measured using the Gini coefficient calculated on the vector of individual player minutes. A higher Gini indicates greater inequality with few players receiving most minutes while lower values suggest more equal distribution. These Gini coefficients are averaged across all playoff team seasons to produce a league wide measure.

Step 11: A heatmap is constructed with years on the horizontal axis and teams on the vertical axis where each cell shows the continuity ratio for that team season combination. Only teams with playoff appearances in the intersected years are included in the visualization. The maximum continuity value across all cells is extracted as a summary statistic.

Step 12: Recent stability is tested by comparing continuity in the most recent three seasons to the prior three seasons using a two sided Welch t test. The test is only performed if at least six seasons exist otherwise the verdict is set to zero by rule. The binary verdict equals one only if the recent mean exceeds the prior mean and the p value is below five percent indicating statistically significant increase.

Highest Continuity Ratio: 1.0000

KEY FINDINGS:

The cross validated AUC of 0.7007 indicates moderate discrimination ability when predicting playoff participation from regular season statistics. The negative coefficient on minutes reflects multicollinearity with games played and points where higher minutes conditional on the other variables suggests lower efficiency. The first principal component captures 89.46 percent of variance showing that games played, minutes, and points are highly correlated and nearly redundant. The single score model achieves 0.6775 AUC demonstrating that a composite performance measure provides reasonable but slightly reduced predictive power compared to using all three variables. The average continuity of 0.9994 reveals that playoff rosters are nearly identical to regular season rosters with minimal midseason acquisitions. The Gini coefficient of 0.4068 indicates moderate concentration where some players receive substantially more playoff minutes than others. The stability verdict of zero shows no significant increase in recent continuity compared to prior years suggesting roster management practices have remained consistent.
