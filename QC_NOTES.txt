QUALITY CONTROL NOTES FOR HAPPINESS ANALYSIS 2015 TO 2017

PROMPT GOAL

The objective was to analyze happiness metrics across three years of country level data from 2015 2016 and 2017 CSV files containing various wellbeing indicators. The analysis required standardizing column names across years with different naming conventions to create a unified dataset for statistical modeling. A two way fixed effects regression with country and year indicators was needed to explain happiness scores using economic health and social predictors with cluster robust standard errors by country. Additional tasks included computing correlation measures between economic factors and happiness rankings calculating year over year changes performing principal components analysis on standardized predictors and conducting hypothesis testing. The analysis also required visualization of temporal trends and answering a theoretical question about PELT penalty choice in changepoint detection. All results needed precise rounding to four decimal places with specific handling rules for edge cases like insufficient data.

STEP BY STEP SOLUTION

Step 1: The first step involved loading the three CSV files for years 2015 2016 and 2017 into separate dataframes using pandas read csv function. Each file contained country level happiness data but with inconsistent column naming conventions across years requiring careful examination. The data loading phase ensured all files were accessible and ready for preprocessing before any transformations.

Step 2: Column standardization was performed by renaming variables to consistent names across all three years where 2015 and 2016 shared identical column titles but 2017 used dot notation instead of parentheses. All happiness related metrics were renamed to use underscores such as converting Happiness Score to Happiness Score and Economy GDP per Capita to Economy GDP per Capita maintaining exact correspondence. The standardization also included creating a Year variable for each dataset to enable pooled cross sectional time series analysis.

Step 3: The datasets were filtered to retain only the essential columns including Country Happiness Score the six predictor variables Happiness Rank Dystopia Residual and the newly created Year indicator. This column selection ensured that each annual cross section contained identical variables for valid comparison and pooling. The three filtered dataframes were then concatenated vertically to create a unified pooled dataset with observations from all three years.

Step 4: For the two way fixed effects regression the pooled data was prepared by removing any rows with missing values in the dependent variable or predictors to ensure complete cases. Country dummy variables were created using one hot encoding with the first country dropped as reference category to avoid perfect multicollinearity. Similarly year dummy variables were generated with one year as baseline allowing the model to capture both cross sectional country heterogeneity and temporal year effects simultaneously.

Step 5: The fixed effects model was estimated using ordinary least squares with the design matrix combining the six continuous predictors country fixed effects and year fixed effects. Cluster robust standard errors were computed grouping by country to account for within country correlation across years which is critical for valid inference in panel data. The within R squared statistic was calculated by demeaning both actual and fitted happiness scores by country means then computing the ratio of explained to total within country variance.

Step 6: Pearson correlation analysis examined the linear relationship between Economy GDP per Capita and Happiness Score using the pooled dataset after filtering for complete observations on both variables. The correlation coefficient measured the strength and direction of association between economic prosperity and subjective wellbeing across all countries and years. A threshold rule was implemented where if fewer than two matched observations existed the correlation would default to zero by design.

Step 7: Kendall rank correlation was computed specifically for happiness rankings between years 2016 and 2017 by performing an inner merge on country names to identify exact matches. The nonparametric Kendall tau statistic measured the ordinal association between rank positions across consecutive years indicating stability in country relative standings. The analysis included a safeguard setting tau to zero if fewer than two countries overlapped between the two years.

Step 8: Year over year absolute changes in happiness scores were calculated by creating two separate merged datasets pairing 2015 with 2016 and 2016 with 2017 on exact country string matches. For each pair the absolute difference in happiness scores was computed representing volatility magnitude regardless of direction. These two sets of changes were pooled together and the median was calculated as a robust measure of central tendency for typical happiness fluctuations with zero assigned if no paired countries existed.

Step 9: The count of countries present across all three years was determined using set intersection operations on unique country names from each annual dataset. This required exact string equality matching to identify only those countries with complete temporal coverage. The integer count provided insight into panel balance and the extent of consistent global coverage across the study period.

Step 10: Principal components analysis was performed on the six standardized predictor variables after removing incomplete cases and applying z score standardization to ensure equal weighting. The first principal component represented the dominant linear combination explaining maximum variance in the predictor space capturing the primary dimension of cross country variation. The percentage of total variance explained by this first component was extracted with a rule to return zero if predictors were entirely missing after filtering indicating data quality issues.

Step 11: Temporal visualization was created by calculating mean happiness scores for each year across all available countries then plotting these three annual means against time. The plot used year on the x axis and mean happiness on the y axis with markers and lines to show the trend trajectory from 2015 through 2017. The highest of the three yearly means was identified and reported to four decimal places as requested.

Step 12: The final statistical test involved a paired t test comparing happiness scores between 2015 and 2017 for countries present in both years using exact country name matching. The difference was calculated as 2017 minus 2015 values and tested against the null hypothesis of zero mean change using a two sided alternative. A binary verdict indicator was constructed equaling one only if the mean difference was positive and the p value fell strictly below the 0.05 threshold otherwise zero representing no significant increase in happiness over the period.
