PROMPT GOAL

The primary objective is to conduct a formal analytical study examining equity behavior patterns across three distinct stock datasets representing ABNB, ABBV, and AAL with standardized date formats and cleaned numeric values. The analysis requires computing daily logarithmic returns while excluding zero volume trading days and ensuring all three datasets participate in every analytical component with their designated roles. A comprehensive modeling framework establishes ARIMA forecasting for ABNB, contemporaneous return correlation between ABBV and AAL, intraday volatility proxies, volume return relationships, comparative price levels, temporal variability measures, trading behavior patterns, and tail distribution characteristics. Each analytical component must generate specific numerical outputs rounded to designated decimal places and produce three distinct visualizations including a line plot for ABNB prices, a scatter plot for aligned returns, and a boxplot for daily ranges. The final deliverable includes nine key quantitative results with precise rounding specifications and a market behavior interpretation paragraph embedded within the analysis script explaining the observed patterns in terms of liquidity, volatility clustering, systematic risk factors, information arrival, and price discovery mechanisms.


STEP BY STEP SOLUTION

Step 1: The analysis begins by loading three equity datasets from CSV files containing Date, Open, High, Low, Close, Adjusted Close, and Volume fields for ABNB, ABBV, and AAL stocks. Each dataset undergoes robust data cleaning where the Date column is parsed into a consistent datetime format to enable temporal alignment across all subsequent operations. Any rows containing missing values or non numeric entries in the required fields are systematically removed to ensure analytical integrity, and the cleaned datasets are sorted chronologically by Date to maintain proper time series ordering.

Step 2: Daily returns are calculated for each stock using the natural logarithm transformation applied to the ratio of consecutive Adjusted Close values, which provides a symmetric measure of price changes suitable for statistical analysis. The calculation specifically uses the formula of log of current day Adjusted Close minus log of previous day Adjusted Close to derive continuously compounded returns. Trading days with zero Volume are explicitly excluded from the return series for each stock to eliminate non trading periods that could distort volatility and correlation measures.

Step 3: The first analytical component implements an autoregressive integrated moving average model with orders one one one fitted exclusively to the ABNB Adjusted Close price series sorted by Date. The fitted ARIMA model generates a forecast for the subsequent thirty trading days beyond the available data, capturing short term momentum and mean reversion dynamics present in the historical price series. The arithmetic mean of these thirty forecasted Adjusted Close values is computed and reported rounded to two decimal places as 133.37, while a line plot visualizes the entire ABNB adjusted price series with Date on the x axis and Adjusted Closing Value on the y axis.

Step 4: The second component measures the strength of contemporaneous association between ABBV and AAL daily returns by aligning both return series on their common trading dates. A Spearman rank correlation coefficient is computed from these aligned return pairs, providing a non parametric measure of monotonic relationship that is robust to outliers and non normality in return distributions. The resulting correlation value of 0.1910 rounded to four decimals indicates a weak positive association, and a scatter plot displays ABBV Daily Return on the x axis against AAL Daily Return on the y axis to visualize this relationship.

Step 5: Intraday dispersion characteristics are examined by calculating the daily range as the difference between High and Low prices for each trading day across all three datasets. The mean of these daily ranges is computed separately for ABNB, ABBV, and AAL, yielding values of 6.3341, 1.5356, and 0.9090 respectively, which serve as volatility proxies reflecting typical intraday price movement magnitudes. These three mean range values are then averaged to produce an overall volatility measure of 2.9262 rounded to four decimals, and a boxplot visualizes the distribution of daily ranges with Ticker Symbol on the x axis and Daily High minus Low Value on the y axis.

Step 6: The relationship between trading activity and return magnitude is explored by constructing simple linear regressions for each dataset where the dependent variable is the absolute value of daily returns and the independent variable is the natural logarithm of Volume. Separate regression models are fitted for ABNB, ABBV, and AAL, yielding slope coefficients of 0.0231, 0.0128, and 0.0063 respectively, which quantify how much absolute return tends to increase per unit increase in log transformed trading volume. The mean of these three regression slopes is calculated as 0.0141 rounded to four decimals, representing the average volume return sensitivity across the three stocks.

Step 7: A comparison of general price levels is conducted by first identifying the intersection of all trading dates that appear in all three datasets, resulting in 699 common dates representing the shared observation period. The median Adjusted Close value is computed for each stock using only these common dates, producing medians of 141.77 for ABNB, 135.36 for ABBV, and 16.59 for AAL. The arithmetic mean of these three median values is calculated as 97.91 rounded to two decimals, providing a central tendency measure of typical price levels across the three equities during their overlapping trading period.

Step 8: Short horizon variability is evaluated by computing a thirty day rolling window standard deviation of daily returns for each stock, creating a time series that captures how return volatility evolves over successive thirty day periods. The maximum value attained by each rolling standard deviation series is extracted, yielding peaks of 0.0534 for ABNB, 0.0464 for ABBV, and 0.1472 for AAL, which identify the most volatile thirty day windows experienced by each stock. These three maximum rolling standard deviations are averaged to produce 0.0823 rounded to four decimals, representing the typical magnitude of peak short term volatility episodes across the three equities.

Step 9: Temporal trading behavior is examined by calculating the mean daily Volume for each dataset across all trading days, resulting in average volumes of 6679618.46 for ABNB, 7612707.38 for ABBV, and 14821724.49 for AAL. These three mean Volume values are then averaged to produce 9704683.44 rounded to two decimals, representing the typical daily trading activity level across the three stocks. This measure reflects the overall liquidity and investor interest in the analyzed equities during their respective observation periods.

Step 10: Distinctive tail behavior is assessed by computing the width of the return distribution tails for each stock using the difference between the ninetieth percentile and tenth percentile of daily returns calculated from the aligned sample of common dates. The tail width values are 0.0828 for ABNB, 0.0350 for ABBV, and 0.0630 for AAL, reflecting the spread of typical versus extreme return outcomes. These three tail width measures are averaged to produce 0.0603 rounded to four decimals, and separately the maximum absolute value among the three volume return regression slopes is identified as 0.0231, indicating that ABNB exhibits the strongest volume return relationship among the three equities.
