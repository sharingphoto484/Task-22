COEFFICIENT OF VARIATION ERRORS

The model reported 0.2160 for the 2015 coefficient of variation of Score, but the correct value is 0.2167, likely because the model used population standard deviation with n divisor instead of sample standard deviation with n minus 1 divisor, or failed to properly filter the dataset to include only the 144 common countries present across all three years. This discrepancy of 0.0007 suggests systematic underestimation of variability, possibly due to incorrect degrees of freedom in the standard deviation calculation or inclusion of countries that should have been excluded during the intersection filtering step. The error propagated through subsequent calculations affecting the mean coefficient of variation and compromising the assessment of Score dispersion in the 2015 dataset.

The model reported 0.2121 for the 2017 coefficient of variation of Score, but the correct value is 0.2128, likely because the model applied inconsistent data cleaning procedures such as retaining rows with missing values in non critical columns or using a different country intersection set than the 144 nations present in all three years. This error of 0.0007 indicates potential issues with the standardization pipeline, possibly stemming from improper handling of the common countries filter or using different rounding precision in intermediate standard deviation and mean calculations. The systematic underestimation pattern across years suggests the model may have used biased variance estimators or failed to properly subset the data before computing descriptive statistics.

The model reported 0.2038 for the 2019 coefficient of variation of Score, but the correct value is 0.2045, likely because the model computed standard deviation using the wrong denominator in the variance formula or included countries beyond the 144 common nations identified through set intersection. This discrepancy of 0.0007 mirrors the errors in 2015 and 2017, strongly suggesting a systematic bias in the variance calculation methodology rather than random computational errors. The consistent underestimation across all three years indicates the model likely used population variance with n divisor instead of sample variance with n minus 1 divisor, which is the appropriate estimator for finite samples drawn from a larger population.

The model reported 0.2106 for the mean coefficient of variation across all three years, but the correct value is 0.2114, likely because the model averaged the three incorrectly computed individual year coefficients of variation of 0.2160, 0.2121, and 0.2038 instead of using the correct values of 0.2167, 0.2128, and 0.2045. This error of 0.0008 in the mean CV directly results from the propagation of the systematic underestimation errors in each yearly coefficient of variation calculation. The compounding effect of using biased variance estimators across all three years led to an overall mean that underestimates the true relative variability in wellbeing scores throughout the study period, potentially misleading interpretations about temporal trends in Score dispersion.
